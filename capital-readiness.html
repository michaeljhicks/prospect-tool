STEP 4 OF 5 · OPTIONAL FINANCIAL SNAPSHOT
Your Financial SnapshotExpand to explore liquidity, assets, liabilities, income and financing questions. Not required.＋

This section is for your own review. Enter numbers only if you want to. Fields left blank remain unknown; zero is a deliberate value. Entries are not saved, uploaded or submitted to Iceworks. Don't enter identity numbers or account details.

Income, available budget & financing context

Enter the total amount you earn over a year before taxes from employment and other income sources you choose to include. For example, a $90,000 annual salary means entering $90,000. Income may provide financial context but is not cash immediately available to invest.
Enter the total amount you would feel comfortable committing to the business, even if you would need financing to reach it. For example, you might intend to invest $25,000 but only have $10,000 accessible today. The tool will flag that difference rather than treating your intention as cash.
An intention, not proof of available funds
Enter the amount of funding you're exploring but have not yet secured, such as a loan application or a possible credit line. This amount does not increase your available capital until the funding is actually approved and available, at which point you can enter it in Step 1.
Not counted as committed funding
Select where you are in obtaining additional financing: researching options, applied, conditionally preapproved, or fully approved and available. Conditional preapproval is not spendable money. When funding is truly committed and accessible, enter its amount in Step 1.

Assets — keep spendable funds separate

Enter the total balance of your checking accounts, savings accounts, and similar cash holdings, including any money you intend to keep for personal reserves. This is your overall cash picture, not just the portion you'd invest. Do not add this a second time to Step 1.
Enter the approximate current value of investments OUTSIDE retirement accounts, such as stocks, bonds, or mutual funds in a regular brokerage account. They may fluctuate or require selling before you can use the money, so the tool doesn't automatically treat them as available business cash.
Enter the approximate current value of retirement or otherwise restricted accounts, such as a 401(k), IRA, or similar retirement savings. These assets may contribute to net worth but are not necessarily accessible without taxes, penalties, or other restrictions. They are not counted as funding for Iceworks.
Enter what your house, land, rental property, or other real estate could reasonably SELL FOR today, before subtracting any mortgage. It is not the price you paid and not the equity you own. Example: a home worth $400,000 with a $250,000 mortgage has a $400,000 market value; enter the $250,000 mortgage separately under liabilities.
Enter the approximate current resale value of valuable things you own that are not listed above, such as vehicles or an ownership stake in another business. Use what you could reasonably sell them for today, not what you originally paid. Avoid counting an asset twice.

Liabilities and personal obligations

Enter the total amount of mortgage PRINCIPAL you still owe on your home, land, or investment properties. For example, if a home could sell for $400,000 and you still owe $250,000, enter $250,000 here. This is the loan balance, not the monthly mortgage payment.
Enter the total outstanding balances on credit cards, auto loans, student loans, personal loans, and other debts not listed as mortgages. This is the amount still owed, not the amount of the monthly payments. Don't include a mortgage already entered above.
Enter money you owe that doesn't fit into the mortgage or other-loan categories—for example, an outstanding tax obligation or another documented debt. Use the remaining balance, not the monthly payment, and don't count an obligation twice.
Enter the TOTAL of required monthly payments on your personal debts, such as mortgage, car loan, student loan, and minimum credit-card payments. Example: $1,800 mortgage + $400 car + $100 credit card = $2,300 per month. Do not enter your total debt balance, groceries, or other ordinary living expenses here.
For context only, not a lending decision
If you have previously calculated or reported a net-worth figure on a separate form, enter that total here. The tool compares it with the assets minus liabilities entered above and flags any difference. Leave it empty if you have never reported a number; a negative value is allowed.
